Research

I develop knowledge-informed machine learning for complex scientific and physical systems. In medicine, agriculture, and manufacturing, labeled data are expensive, every patient, field, or production line is a little different, and a wrong decision is costly. Rather than asking AI to learn everything from data, I design models that start from what domain experts already know and use data to learn the rest. This makes them data-efficient, robust when conditions shift, and useful for real decisions.

  • Knowledge as a priorAnatomy, soil–water–crop physics, and process structure are encoded into model architecture, losses, and initialization.
  • Modern AI as the engineFoundation models, LLMs, generative models (ControlNet), and world models provide flexible capacity.
  • Decisions as the goalModels are judged by the quality of the diagnoses and control actions they enable, with theory linking prediction error to decision regret.

Research Thrusts

01

Perception: learning to see with few labels

Semi-supervised learningGenerative AIFoundation models3D medical imaging

In plain words

A dentist can spot a lesion on a 3D scan because they know where teeth, bone, and nerves should be. I give AI that same anatomical sense, so it can learn from a small number of expert-labeled scans.

What I do

I integrate oral-anatomical knowledge into semi-supervised 3D segmentation of cone-beam CT (CBCT), normalize radiomic features by anatomy to separate lesion types, reconstruct pseudo-healthy brain MRI with an edge-guided ControlNet, and cascade foundation models for zero-shot microscopy analysis in cell-therapy manufacturing. Two methods are protected by U.S. provisional patents and developed with clinicians.

Reasoning & prediction: foundation models for scientific signals

LLMsSpatiotemporal learningAnomaly detectionPrecision agriculture

In plain words

Soil sensors produce streams of numbers that only an expert can read. I build models that read these streams like an agronomist, recognizing what is happening underground and anticipating what it means for the harvest.

What I do

I develop LLM-based frameworks that combine domain knowledge with sensor data for soil-moisture pattern recognition and anomaly detection, learn depth-specific soil-moisture trajectories to predict yield across years, and build lightweight anomaly detection for on-site nutrient management and multi-farm decision support.

03

Decision & control: adaptive world models

World modelsMeta-learningTest-time adaptationModel predictive control

In plain words

Before irrigating, a good farmer imagines how the crop will respond. A world model is that imagination for an AI. My work lets it adapt to a new farm by reading a description of the farm’s soil and climate and then fine-tuning from just a few sensor readings.

What I do

In KIWIC, an action-conditioned world model learns shared soil–water–crop dynamics. A lightweight adapter captures what is specific to each environment: an LLM encodes environmental knowledge, a hypernetwork turns it into the adapter’s initialization, meta-learning with factual and counterfactual irrigation responses teaches the adapter how to adapt, and test-time adaptation refines it from sparse soil-moisture observations. The adapted model then drives model predictive control. I also analyze why prior knowledge matters when sparse observations leave the dynamics only partially identifiable, and derive a regret bound that connects prediction error to control decisions.

Roots in manufacturing analytics

Data miningQuality & reliability

My earlier work at Yonsei University developed a numerical association-rule mining method that pinpoints which tool groups introduce faults in a printed circuit board production line (International Journal of Production Research, 2024). It started my interest in turning industrial data into actionable engineering knowledge.

Where This Research Is Going

From simulation to the field

Validate adaptive world models on working farms, adding remote sensing and plant-based measurements so adaptation stays reliable when observations are sparse.

Scientific world models across systems

Generalize knowledge-conditioned adaptation across crops, sites, and other physical systems such as manufacturing processes, so one model can quickly specialize to many environments.

AI for clinical decisions

Move from detecting disease to predicting treatment outcomes from 3D imaging, as in my ongoing work on endodontic prognosis and an NIH NIDCR proposal for CBCT clinical decision support.
